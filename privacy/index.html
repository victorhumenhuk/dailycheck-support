DailyCheck privacy notice

Effective September 18, 2026

Who we are

DailyCheck is provided by Thesmios, which is the controller of personal information processed for DailyCheck where it decides why and how that information is used.

For product help or feedback, email dailycheck@thesmios.com. Privacy questions, data-rights requests, complaints and security reports use the same address, dailycheck@thesmios.com.

What DailyCheck is

DailyCheck is a private daily check-in for families who live apart. One person taps once a day to say they are okay. The people they chose see that check-in, privately, and nobody else does.

DailyCheck does not monitor anyone. It has no location tracking, no health records, no medication tracking, no mood scoring, no streaks and no activity feed. It cannot detect that someone is unwell, and it never reports a day without a check-in as a missed one, because it has no way of knowing.

Information DailyCheck uses

Depending on which side of the app you use and what you choose to do:

  • The display name you choose or type, shown with your check-ins so the people you chose know who they are from.
  • The check-ins you send: which of the three choices you made, the time, and an optional note, voice note or photo if you attached one.
  • The people you choose to receive them: a selected or typed name and an optional relationship kept on your iPhone.
  • Your reminder time, if you turn the daily reminder on.
  • Your household home time zone, used to give “today” the same meaning on both phones.
  • Selected Shared updates: appointments and practical requests, such as shopping, a ride or an errand, when you decide to share them.
  • On the family phone: received check-ins, connection information and the notification and privacy settings the family member chooses.
  • An optional private recovery copy: a versioned snapshot and separate voice or photo assets stored in your private CloudKit space.

DailyCheck contains no advertising network, no analytics, and no third-party software development kit of any kind. It does not sell personal information.

Where information is kept

Your records are stored on your iPhone in an encrypted local store, sealed with a key held in the iPhone’s keychain and reachable only while the device is unlocked. That store is excluded from the general iCloud backup.

Voice notes and photos are stored the same way, on the device, until they are attached to a check-in you choose to send.

Family check-in history is kept locally on the family member’s iPhone. A local export leaves the app only when the person chooses a destination in the system share sheet.

Your setup — the people you chose, private zone names, home time zone and reminder configuration — can sync between your own iPhones through Apple’s iCloud key-value store, which is private to your Apple Account. Your check-ins themselves are not written there.

If you choose private recovery, DailyCheck stores a versioned snapshot and separate voice and photo assets in a private CloudKit recovery zone. It is not a developer backup service. It is available only through your Apple Account.

When information leaves the device

DailyCheck uses Apple’s iCloud for private sharing and optional recovery. DailyCheck has no application server or relay. It does not send your check-ins to a developer-operated service.

You can also choose to share a setup request or export using the system share sheet. The destination you choose handles that information under its own terms. If you send support or feedback email, Thesmios receives the information you include through email providers. Do not include private check-in content unless you want to share it with support.

When you send a check-in, the following are placed in a separate private CloudKit space for each person you chose, and for nobody else:

  • the choice you made and the time you made it;
  • your display name;
  • an optional note, voice note or photo, if you attached one; and
  • an appointment or practical request you chose to share.

Each person has their own private space. One person can never see another person’s, and none of them can see anything else on your iPhone.

A photo is resized and re-encoded on your iPhone before it is sent, which also removes any location the camera recorded in it. DailyCheck never receives access to your photo library: the system picker hands over only the single image you chose. The microphone is used only while you are recording a voice note, and only after you grant permission.

Why we use information and the legal basis

  • Performing our contract with you: storing your check-ins, delivering them to the people you chose and running your reminder.
  • Our legitimate interests: keeping the app secure and working correctly, and answering the support and privacy messages you send us.
  • Your consent, where the operating system asks for it: notifications and microphone access. You can withdraw either in iPhone Settings at any time.

How long information is kept

Family check-in history is available for 90 days without a subscription. A household chooses whether to keep its own local check-in history for 30 days, 90 days or a year.

Stopping a person’s access removes their private share, and their cached copy is cleared the next time they open DailyCheck. It cannot retract anything they have already read or saved.

Deleting everything stops every person’s access first, confirmed with iCloud, before removing anything locally. It clears your check-ins, people, appointments, requests, reminder settings, local family preferences, recovery state, voice notes and photos.

Your rights and choices

Subject to applicable law, you can ask to access, correct, erase, restrict, receive or object to processing of your personal information, and withdraw consent where consent is the basis for processing.

You can export a complete copy of everything DailyCheck holds about you at any time, from Settings, without asking us.

Email dailycheck@thesmios.com to make a request. We may need to confirm your identity before acting on it.

If you are unhappy with how we have handled your information, you can complain to the Information Commissioner’s Office at ico.org.uk.

International transfers and processors

Private sharing and recovery use Apple’s iCloud and CloudKit. Optional support email also passes through email providers. Thesmios operates no servers of its own.
